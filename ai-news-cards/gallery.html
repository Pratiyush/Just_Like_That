AI News Card Kit

Instagram card designs for AI news. Each one is built around named attention research: what makes people stop scrolling, read, save, send and comment. Tap a card for the reasoning, a headline formula and a caption tip.

01

Breaking Alert

Stop the scroll

Breaking announcements, model launches, surprise releases, major industry events: anything that is new right now and worth sending to someone.

Pre-attentive processing (Treisman & Gelade, 1980) · Isolation (Von Restorff) effect (von Restorff, 1933) · Urgency and timeliness (scarcity of time, one of Cialdini's levers, 1984) · High-arousal emotion drives sharing (Berger & Milkman, 2012)

02

Stat Bomb

Stop the scroll

Benchmarks, funding rounds, user counts, cost drops: any story where one number is the news. Shorter stats set bigger, so write "$4.2B" rather than "$4,200,000,000". Currency signs, %, ×, and unit letters (B, M, K, hrs) are automatically set smaller so the digits get the scale.

Isolation (Von Restorff) effect · Pre-attentive size contrast · Hick's law (one focal point)

03

Terminal

Get sent

Model releases, dev tools, open-source drops and API launches: news a developer would forward to their team chat.

STEPPS: social currency and in-group identity · Novelty seeking · Information-gap curiosity · Isolation (Von Restorff) effect

04

Swiss Editorial

Build trust

Serious analysis: research findings and their implications, policy and regulation, benchmark audits, 'what this actually means' explainers.

Processing fluency (Reber & Schwarz, 1999) · Authority (Cialdini, 1984) · Aesthetic-usability effect (Kurosu & Kashimura, 1995) · F-shaped scanning (Nielsen, 2006 eye-tracking)

05

Aurora Glass

Stop the scroll

Product and model launches, new features and capability upgrades: news that should feel like the future just arrived.

Aesthetic-usability effect (Kurosu & Kashimura, 1995) · Awe drives sharing (Berger & Milkman, 2012) · Figure-ground (Gestalt; Wertheimer, 1923) · First-impression speed (Lindgaard et al., 2006)

06

Neo-Brutal

Drive comments

Hot takes, opinions, contrarian calls and quirky AI news that people will want to argue about or send to a friend.

Novelty seeking / pattern interrupt (Bunzeck & Düzel, 2006) · Isolation (Von Restorff) effect (von Restorff, 1933) · Social currency (STEPPS; Berger, Contagious, 2013) · Gestalt continuation and common region (Wertheimer, 1923; Palmer, 1992)

07

Myth vs Fact

Get sent

Debunking hype and common misconceptions about AI: what detectors, chatbots, bigger models or 'AI will take every job' claims can and can't actually do.

Cognitive dissonance / expectation violation (Festinger, 1957) · Contrast and isolation (Von Restorff effect, von Restorff, 1933) · Information-gap curiosity (Loewenstein, 1994) · Practical value drives sends and saves (STEPPS, Berger, 2013)

08

Quote Card

Build trust

Attributable quotes from researchers, founders and policymakers: keynote lines, interview pull-quotes, hearing testimony, a paper author explaining what a result means.

Authority (Cialdini, 1984) · Stories / human voice (STEPPS: Berger, Contagious, 2013) · Picture superiority / dual coding (Paivio, 1971) · Processing fluency (Reber & Schwarz, 1999)

09

Cheat Sheet

Earn saves

Tool lists, prompt tips and how-tos ('5 AI tools that...', 'Stop typing X, do this instead'). Works with 3 to 6 points.

STEPPS: practical value (Berger, 2013) · Chunking (Miller, 1956; Cowan, 2001) · Serial position effect (Murdock, 1962) · Picture superiority / dual coding (Paivio, 1971)

10

Carousel Hook

Hold attention

The first slide of a multi-slide explainer: a model doing something unexpected, a research result unpacked step by step, a 'how it works' breakdown or a ranked list whose answers live on the following slides.

Zeigarnik effect / open loops (Zeigarnik, 1927) · Information-gap curiosity (Loewenstein, 1994) · Gestalt continuation (Wertheimer, 1923) · High-arousal emotion drives sharing (Berger & Milkman, 2012)

11

Front Page

Stop the scroll

Historic milestones and 'this changes everything' stories: a model doing something for the first time, a record broken, a launch people will remember the date of.

Novelty seeking / expectation violation (Bunzeck & Düzel, 2006) · Authority (Cialdini, 1984) · Nostalgia as a positive emotion (Wildschut, Sedikides, Arndt & Routledge, 2006) · Picture superiority / dual coding (Paivio, 1971)

12

Neon Grid

Stop the scroll

Robotics, AGI talk, futuristic breakthroughs and AI hardware (chips, humanoids, compute): news that should feel like the future arriving. The stat is printed on the sun, so keep it to about 5 characters ("4:12", "40×", "1M"); longer values still fit but shrink. Long headlines automatically switch to sentence case and lower the horizon to give the copy more sky.

Novelty seeking · High-arousal emotion drives sharing · Pre-attentive processing · Pattern interrupt

13

Minimal Luxe

Build trust

Major announcements, premium brand moments and a single profound line: a flagship model launch, a landmark result, a milestone, a year-in-review statement.

Isolation (Von Restorff) effect · Hick's law · Aesthetic-usability effect · Processing fluency

14

Data Story

Build trust

Benchmark results, adoption trends, cost and price curves, latency or efficiency comparisons: any story where one number in a short series (4 to 8 values) is the news.

Picture superiority / dual coding (Paivio, 1971) · Pre-attentive processing (Treisman & Gelade, 1980) · Isolation (Von Restorff) effect (von Restorff, 1933) · Authority: credibility of data (Cialdini, 1984)

15

Versus

Drive comments

Model vs model, company vs company, open vs closed, human vs AI: any story that is really a head-to-head with two or three comparable numbers per side.

Social identity / in-group bias (Tajfel & Turner, 1979) · Pre-attentive processing via complementary colour contrast (Treisman & Gelade, 1980) · Contrast principle (Cialdini, 1984) · Commitment & consistency (Cialdini, 1984)

16

Pop Comic

Get sent

Surprising, funny or weird AI news: agents doing absurd things, robots with unexpected habits, delightful failures and 'wait, really?' research results.

Gaze cueing (Friesen & Kingstone, 1998) · Amusement drives sharing (Berger & Milkman, 2012) · Novelty / pattern interrupt (Bunzeck & Düzel, 2006) · Picture superiority / dual coding (Paivio, 1971)

17

Hot Question

Drive comments

Ethical debates, predictions and "would you...?" prompts sparked by an AI news story: anything where people will happily pick a side in the comments.

Self-reference effect (Rogers, Kuiper & Kirker, 1977) · Commitment & consistency (Cialdini, 1984) · Information-gap curiosity (Loewenstein, 1994) · Comments as an engagement signal · Isolation (Von Restorff) effect

18

Bento Digest

Earn saves

Weekly roundups and "5 things that happened in AI this week" digests: 4–6 short stories, each boiled down to a tag and a one-line takeaway.

Chunking (Miller, 1956; Cowan, 2001) · Gestalt common region & proximity (Palmer, 1992; Wertheimer, 1923) · Practical value drives saves (STEPPS; Berger, 2013) · First-impression speed (Lindgaard et al., 2006)

19

Hazard

Get sent

AI scams, deepfakes, voice cloning, privacy and security risks: any story where the reader can do something concrete to protect themselves or the people they care about.

Negativity bias (Baumeister et al., 2001) · Loss aversion (Kahneman & Tversky, 1979) · Yellow/black as a pre-attentive warning signal (aposematism; Treisman & Gelade, 1980) · Practical value drives sends (STEPPS: Berger, Contagious, 2013)

20

Blueprint

Stop the scroll

Research paper breakdowns and technical explainers: a new architecture, training trick or benchmark result where 'how it works' is the hook.

Picture superiority / dual coding (Paivio, 1971) · Authority of a scientific aesthetic (Cialdini, 1984) · Information-gap curiosity (Loewenstein, 1994) · Chunking (Miller, 1956; Cowan, 2001)

Make a card from your news

Every design reads the same JSON fields, so one story can be rendered in all 20 styles and you pick the strongest.

  1. Write the story as JSON

    Use the shared fields. Unused fields are ignored; missing ones fall back to the sample.

    {
  "kicker": "NEW MODEL",
  "headline": "Your hook in under 12 words",
  "highlight": "under 12 words",
  "subhead": "One line of context.",
  "stat": "10×", "statLabel": "cheaper",
  "source": "Source: the lab's blog",
  "date": "Nov 9, 2026",
  "handle": "@yourhandle"
}

  2. Render

    Playwright opens each template and saves a 1080 × 1350 PNG.

    npm install
npx playwright install chromium
node render.js --data my-story.json --sheet

  3. Pick and post

    Compare the contact sheet, post the winner, and use the card's caption tip. Recolour any design with "theme": { "--accent": "#00E5FF" }.

    node render.js --data my-story.json --only 02,09

The research behind the designs

The principles these cards apply, where each comes from, and which designs use it. Tap a number to open that card.

PrincipleOriginOn a cardUsed in

Pre-attentive processing

Treisman & Gelade, 1980

Colour, size and orientation register before reading starts, so one strong contrast stops the thumb.

Isolation (Von Restorff) effect

von Restorff, 1933

The one element that differs gets noticed and remembered.

First impressions in ~50 ms

Lindgaard et al., 2006

Visual appeal is judged before any words are read.

Information-gap curiosity

Loewenstein, 1994

A specific gap in knowledge creates an itch to close it.

Zeigarnik effect / open loops

Zeigarnik, 1927

Unfinished things nag, so a "1/5" or cut-off shape pulls the next swipe.

Processing fluency

Reber & Schwarz, 1999

Text that is easy to read feels more true and more likeable.

High-arousal emotion

Berger & Milkman, 2012

Awe, excitement, amusement and anxiety get shared more than calm content.

STEPPS: social currency & practical value

Berger, 2013

People share what makes them look informed and what is useful to others.

Negativity bias & loss aversion

Baumeister et al., 2001; Kahneman & Tversky, 1979

Threats and potential losses grab attention faster than gains.

Picture superiority / dual coding

Paivio, 1971

Image plus words is remembered better than words alone.

Chunking

Miller, 1956; Cowan, 2001

Working memory holds about four chunks, so 3 to 5 items per card.

Serial position effect

Murdock, 1962

First and last items in a list are remembered best.

Gestalt grouping

Wertheimer, 1923; Palmer, 1992

Proximity, common region and continuation organise a card at a glance.

Gaze cueing

Friesen & Kingstone, 1998

Eyes, even cartoon ones, pull attention where they look.

Self-reference effect

Rogers, Kuiper & Kirker, 1977

Information about "you" is processed more deeply.

Cialdini's levers

Cialdini, 1984

Authority, commitment and consistency, and social proof make claims persuasive.

Aesthetic-usability effect

Kurosu & Kashimura, 1995

Beautiful designs are perceived as easier to use and more credible.

Hick's law

Hick, 1952

Fewer elements, faster decisions: one clear focal point.

Novelty & pattern interrupt

Bunzeck & Düzel, 2006

Unexpected formats activate reward circuits and earn a second look.

Social identity

Tajfel & Turner, 1979

Picking a side invites people to defend it in the comments.

Cognitive dissonance

Festinger, 1957

A belief contradicted by a fact creates tension the reader wants to resolve.

F-shaped scanning

Nielsen, 2006

Readers scan the top and left edge first, so that is where the hook goes.

Sample headlines, names and numbers on these cards are fictional placeholders. Replace them with your own verified news before posting. Full-size PNGs are in the repository's output folder.
